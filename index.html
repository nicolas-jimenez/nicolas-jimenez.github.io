About Research Policy Teaching CV

I am a PhD candidate in Economics at Yale University, expecting to complete my degree in May 2027.

I work on development economics and industrial organization. My research studies how governments in developing countries buy goods and infrastructure, and how the rules they use shape which firms compete, which firms grow, and what gets built. My job market paper asks how well Rwanda's procurement system screens construction firms on quality by tying eligibility for future contracts to the execution of past ones — and what that screening costs in lost competition.

Related projects study the supply-chain spillovers of public procurement contracts in Rwanda, corruption and firm competition in Kenyan procurement, and the role of trade fairs in forming firm-to-firm linkages in Chile.
